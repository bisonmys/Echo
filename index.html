Backing

Chord

In Echo the phrase sets the chords. Start the backing and it loops them after each phrase, so you can answer in time and then keep going with your own ideas.

Style

Drone holds root, 3rd and 7th. Comp swings: bass on 1 and 3, rootless chords in a Charleston rhythm. Blocks is Barry Harris: block chords that walk up and down the chord's sixth-diminished scale.

Tempo
100
What Blocks plays here

The sixth chord lands on beats 1 and 3 and the diminished pushes into it from the upbeat, so every chord tone sits on a strong beat. Play that scale over the chord and the same thing happens in your line.

At the top puts higher notes higher on the screen, like the horn in your hands. Toward you turns it to the player's view.

Key size
Fingers

Five-finger is for iPhones, which track five touches. Right-hand keys count the left hand as down, and a low pinky key counts every finger above it. In either mode, Right 3 counts Right 1 and 2. Keys held for you turn dark with a gold ring. The 1&1 A♭ needs Every finger; use bis here.

Sound

Only with Air: fingers pick the note silently and your right thumb plays it. Tap Air to tongue, hold it to slur.

Finger timing

How long the pad waits for fingers to land together. Tight answers fastest. Forgiving hides stray notes between fingerings.

How it works

  • Sing it back first. If you can sing the phrase, your fingers have something to follow. If you can't, tap Hear.
  • The marks fill as you play the right notes in order. Each mark is as long as its note. A wrong note empties them, so you hear exactly where your line went somewhere else.
  • Each note you find says what it is against the chord: the ♭3, the 9, the ♯11. The same degree shows beside the note name as you play it. Tap the phrase to switch found notes between degrees and note names.
  • Want bigger keys? Swipe the bottom panel down, or tap the handle on its top edge. Everything but the phrase, Hear and Next tucks away and the horn takes the screen. Swipe up to bring it back.
  • Can't find where it starts? 1st note lights the key. Show gives you every name.
  • New key moves the same phrase up a fourth. Twelve taps takes it all the way around the horn.
  • Lay the phone flat, mouthpiece end toward you. Left hand comes in from the left, right hand from the right, in the same order as on the horn.
  • Left thumb is the octave key. On its own it stays silent, so you can keep it down while you look for the next note. Air, under your right thumb, plays the all-open note and re-tongues a note you are holding.
  • The G♯ key works here, even though yours is stuck.
  • The two octaves stand side by side. A note and its octave sit at the same height. Flats sit beside the ladder on the side of the hand that plays them.
  • Hold a note and touch another to slur. Lift and touch to tongue. Slide a finger to run across notes.
